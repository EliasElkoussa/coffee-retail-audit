1. Localization and SEO integrity

⌄

The English toggle translates product names but not the structure around them.

What we found

  • Verified9 of 12 main menu items are Arabic-only on the English site, including Gift Boxes, Arabic Coffee, Specialty Coffee, Wholesale and Events.
  • VerifiedMost homepage section headings are Arabic-only, and a leftover template placeholder ("نص فرعي", meaning "subtext") is visible.
  • VerifiedSome products have no English name, and the best-selling Al Kaif Coffee shows "No description".
  • VerifiedProduct records are crossed in the specialty range. One page's web address names Al Dhafra Fort Blend, its heading says Nicaragua Momotombo, and its Google title says Gedeb Halo Hartome. Two more carry English names that don't match their web addresses.
  • VerifiedThe page at /en-ae/ points its canonical tag at /en/, and the language tags omit /en-ae/. Two English addresses compete for one page.
  • VerifiedThe homepage has no main (H1) heading, and category web addresses are Arabic-only.
  • VerifiedThe store description says nine UAE branches plus two in Oman; the homepage lists eight locations.

Why it matters commercially

Every Arabic-only step is a point where an English-interface shopper can drop out, and every English ad click lands on a page that only half-matches the ad. That raises cost per acquisition before any media optimisation starts. Crossed product records also send the wrong product to Google and the ad catalogues.

What to fix

  • Complete Zid's English fields for menus, categories, section headings and every product name and description.
  • Audit every product so its name, web address, Google title and photo describe the same item.
  • Settle on one English address (/en/ or /en-ae/), make it self-canonical and list it in the language tags beside the Arabic version.
  • Add one main heading per page in each language, and correct the branch count everywhere.

2. Homepage and navigation

⌄

The homepage lists most of the catalogue instead of guiding a shopper to a first purchase.

What we found

  • VerifiedThe homepage carries 218 links to product pages.
  • VerifiedCeylon Coffee, Al Majlis Coffee and the logo cup set each appear in at least three homepage sections.
  • Verified12 top-level menu items mix products (coffee, honey, dry food), services (gift wrapping, events) and wholesale.
  • VerifiedTwo homepage sections display "No products".
  • VerifiedAl Kaif lists at AED 95 on cards but its product page opened at AED 115 (1 kg with saffron).

Why it matters commercially

A shopper arriving from an ad has to scan a long, repetitive page to find a first product. Less clarity means fewer add-to-carts per visit, which lowers the return on every dirham of media.

What to fix

  • Homepage order: one hero offer, then four category tiles, best sellers ranked by real sales, gift boxes, branches.
  • Group the menu into Coffee, Tools, Gifts, Pantry and Business (wholesale, events).
  • Remove empty sections and duplicate product rows; open product pages on the variant that matches the card price.

5. Trust signals and checkout friction

⌄

Strong proof of demand, hidden behind empty reviews and an empty shipping page.
  • Verified53 "No reviews" labels on the homepage, next to cards showing 9,000+ units sold.
  • VerifiedThe Payment and Delivery page shows column headers (cities covered, shipping charges) with no rows.
  • VerifiedPayment options shown: Apple Pay, Visa, Mastercard, Amex and cash via store courier. No buy-now-pay-later option is visible.
  • VerifiedProduct pages show a "people viewing this product" counter whose source can't be verified from outside.
  • To verifyWhether shoppers can check out without creating an account.

Why it matters commercially

Shipping cost and delivery time are what a first-time buyer needs to know before checkout. Leaving them blank pushes that buyer to abandon, or to message the store instead.

What to fix

  • Publish delivery zones, fees, any free-shipping threshold and delivery times, in both languages.
  • Invite reviews after delivery, and show ratings on product cards.
  • Show payment options on product pages and in the cart; assess buy-now-pay-later for tools and gift boxes.
  • Keep loyalty points and gift wrapping visible; use only urgency counters based on real data.

6. Media strategy

⌄

Three platforms, automated catalogue campaigns, a simple structure, and scale only what the data proves.
  • HypothesisThis whole section is a position to test on the fixed website. No budgets or results are claimed.

Platform selection

Google (Search, Shopping and YouTube through Performance Max), Meta (Instagram and Facebook) and TikTok. Together they cover search intent and the main discovery feeds, and each has an automated campaign type built for a product catalogue. Adding channels before these are proven would split budget and learning. Once sales justify it, the next step is content, creator and affiliate partnerships, before any new ad channel. UAE first; the wider GCC once delivery outside the UAE is confirmed.

Setup per platform

Starts only once the website fixes are live, because catalogues and tracking read directly from the site.

PlatformProduct catalogueTracking checks
GoogleMerchant Center feed with names, descriptions, prices, stock and shipping in English and ArabicGA4 purchase with order value; Google Ads conversions reviewed, ads_conversion removed as a goal
MetaCommerce Manager catalogue from the same product dataPixel plus Conversions API, each event counted once
TikTokTikTok catalogue from the same product dataPixel plus Events API, same events

Across all three: one naming and tagging convention on every ad, and GA4 as the common yardstick. Zid's ability to sync catalogues and send events to all three platforms needs to be explored; if it doesn't work reliably, that part of the setup gets changed.

Audiences, simplified

Today's automated campaigns find buyers from conversion data and the catalogue rather than hand-built interest lists, so the plan is organised by product category. Broad, always-on ad groups for Arabic coffee, specialty coffee, coffee tools and gifts run inside Performance Max (Google), Advantage+ sales campaigns (Meta) and Smart+ Web Campaigns with Catalog Ads (TikTok). These rotate products automatically. English and Arabic ads run in the same campaigns, each landing on the page in its own language.

Campaign structure

  1. ReadySite fixes live, tracking verified, catalogues approved.
  2. TestOne automated sales campaign per platform, low daily budgets, left alone through the learning phase.
  3. ReadCompare the three platforms on the measures below.
  4. ScaleMove budget to what works, raise it gradually, then extend to the GCC.

What gets evaluated

QuestionMeasures
Where does traffic come from, and is it good?Visits by platform and campaign, engaged visits, bounce
Where do visitors spend time?Category and product views, scroll depth, time on page
Which conversions are driven?Add to cart, checkout starts, purchases, order value, return on ad spend
Where do buyers come from?Emirate or country, device, site language, new or returning

The results then decide the next move: different audiences (least likely, given how the platforms work today), more creative variety including creators and influencers, pricing and offers, or retargeting visitors who didn't buy. Success targets are set from the test baseline, not before.
